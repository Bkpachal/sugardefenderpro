Why the current label matters

Ingredient information in promotional pages can be incomplete, outdated or inconsistent. The most useful source for the version being sold is the current Supplement Facts panel and complete package label. We have not independently verified a complete, current formula for this listing, so we do not publish a definitive ingredient breakdown.

Use this label checklist

  • Ingredient identity: Check the precise botanical or nutrient name, including plant part where listed.
  • Amount per serving: Look for the quantity of each active ingredient, not just a proprietary blend name.
  • Serving size: Confirm how much liquid constitutes one serving and how many servings are in the bottle.
  • Other ingredients: Review sweeteners, preservatives, flavorings and carriers.
  • Allergens and warnings: Check the label for relevant cautions and consult a clinician if unsure.
  • Manufacturer details: Look for a responsible company name and a way to contact it.

Ingredient research is not product proof

A study of one ingredient does not establish that a finished supplement has the same effect. Dose, extract standardization, combinations, product quality and the design of clinical research all matter. Be cautious of claims that imply guaranteed results or treatment of diabetes.

Questions to ask the seller

Ask for a readable image of the latest Supplement Facts panel, confirmation of serving directions, allergen information and any available quality documentation. Keep the response and compare it with the delivered package.
